Care billed per service

Existing balances

Fees above the cap

FAQ

The rules, in plain English.

Does an HSA pay for a DPC membership?

Yes, as of January 1, 2026. Qualifying membership fees are HSA-payable up to $150/month per individual or $300/month per family. To qualify, the membership has to cover primary care only, be delivered by primary care practitioners (family medicine, internal medicine, geriatrics, pediatrics, or NP/PA/CNS), and the fixed fee has to be the only charge inside the arrangement.

Can a membership cost more than $150/month?

Nothing stops you from charging more. But an above-cap arrangement doesn't qualify under the new rules, and patients who actively contribute to an HSA lose the ability to make new contributions while enrolled, however they pay. They can still spend an existing balance on the fee. Practices that want contributing patients to keep everything run a qualifying membership at or under the cap and bill additional care per service, a structure we help set up.

Can patients use an HSA for per-visit payments?

Yes, and they always could. A per-visit charge for medical care is an ordinary qualified medical expense with no cap. The 2026 rules and their $150/$300 limits govern recurring membership fees, not pay-as-you-go care.

Are FSAs eligible?

For per-visit medical charges, yes. For membership fees it's unsettled. The 2026 law changed HSA rules only, and FSA plan administrators disagree on membership fees. That's why we say HSA and not HSA/FSA. Patients should ask their FSA administrator.

What's HSA-eligible with a DPC membership?

Two things. The membership fee, when the arrangement qualifies. And separately billed medical services, meaning visits, labs, and procedures performed outside the membership, in full and with no cap. The $150/$300 limit applies to the periodic fee, not to what a patient spends pre-tax at your practice overall.

What are qualifying services for the HSA?

Qualified services:

  • All primary care practitioner services
  • Preventive care
  • Routine labs
  • In-office procedures
What's excluded from HSA payments?

Excluded:

  • General anesthesia procedures
  • Non-routine labs processed off-site
  • Prescription drugs, apart from vaccines
Does a patient need an HDHP to use HSA money here?

Only to contribute. Spending existing HSA money on qualified expenses works regardless of current insurance, so a patient who left an HDHP years ago can still spend the balance at your practice. And since 2026, bronze and catastrophic exchange plans count as HSA-qualified, so more of your patients can fund an account than before.

What does an HSA-friendly practice look like?

Take a practice charging $325/month all-in today. The HSA-friendly version: a qualifying $150/month membership covering primary care as the only fixed fee, with extended visits, labs, and procedures billed per service as they happen. Contributing patients pay the $150 pre-tax and keep contributing. Every patient covers per-service care from the HSA with no cap. The practice collects on two clean rails we bill automatically. Patients who'd rather stay on a flat above-cap fee can still spend an existing balance on it, after a disclosure they actually read.
