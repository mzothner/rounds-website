FAQ

The rules, in plain English.

Does an HSA pay for a DPC membership?
Yes — as of January 1, 2026. Qualifying membership fees are HSA-eligible up to $150/month per individual or $300/month per family. To qualify, the membership must cover primary care only, be delivered by primary care practitioners (family medicine, internal medicine, geriatrics, pediatrics, or NP/PA/CNS), and the fixed fee must be the only charge inside the arrangement.
Can patients use an HSA for per-visit payments?
Yes — and they always could. A fixed per-visit charge for medical care is an ordinary qualified medical expense with no cap. The 2026 rules and their $150/$300 limits govern recurring membership fees, not pay-as-you-go visits.
Are FSAs eligible?
For per-visit medical charges, yes. For membership fees, it's unsettled — the 2026 law changed HSA rules only, and FSA plan administrators disagree on membership fees. That's why we say HSA, not HSA/FSA. Patients should check with their FSA administrator.
What's HSA-eligible with a DPC membership?
Two things. The membership fee, up to the cap. And separately billed medical services — visits, labs, procedures performed outside the membership — in full, with no cap. The $150/$300 limit applies to the periodic fee, not to a patient's total pre-tax spending at your practice.
What are qualifying services for the HSA?
Qualified services:
  • All primary care practitioner services
  • Preventive care
  • Routine labs
  • In-office procedures
What's excluded from HSA payments?
Excluded:
  • General anesthesia procedures
  • Non-routine labs processed off-site
  • Prescription drugs, apart from vaccines
Does a patient need an HDHP to use HSA money here?
Only to contribute. Spending existing HSA money on qualified expenses works regardless of current insurance — a patient who left an HDHP years ago can still spend their balance at your practice. And since 2026, bronze and catastrophic exchange plans count as HSA-qualified, so more of your patients can fund an HSA than before.
Why does split billing matter, and how does it work?
If a patient pays a membership fee above the cap the wrong way, they lose the ability to make new HSA contributions — and usually find out at tax time. Split billing keeps the compliant portion on the HSA card and the remainder on a second card or bank account, automatically, as one membership. And the line has to be real: separately billed charges stay clean only when they're genuine per-service charges for care actually delivered outside the membership — not a membership fee wearing a different name. Rounds Pay enforces that line for you.
What does an HSA-compliant membership look like?
Take a $325/month concierge primary care membership. Rounds Pay splits it into two charges that run together: $150 on the patient's HSA card, $175 on their regular card or bank account — one membership, fully compliant, cap never breached. When the practice performs additional services — a lab draw, a procedure, an extra visit — those are billed separately and go on the HSA card in full. The patient maximizes pre-tax spending; the practice collects its full fee.
